
# NMOS Transistor Fabrication – Semiconductor Processing & Device Development

## Overview

I am currently working on a senior design project at LeTourneau University focused on developing and fabricating enhancement-mode N-channel MOSFETs (NMOS) using semiconductor processing equipment available in our university laboratory.

The project's primary objective is to establish a repeatable fabrication process capable of producing functioning NMOS transistors from silicon wafers. If successful, the team hopes to expand the process toward fabricating complementary MOS (CMOS) devices.

Unlike projects that focus primarily on circuit simulation, this project involves hands-on semiconductor fabrication, including silicon oxidation, doping, photolithography, wet etching, metal deposition, and electrical characterization.

**Project Duration:** Fall 2026 – Spring 2027

**Current Status:** In Progress

---

## Project Objectives

The primary objectives of this project include:

- Develop a repeatable semiconductor fabrication process using university laboratory equipment.
- Fabricate enhancement-mode NMOS transistors from silicon wafers.
- Develop controlled N-type regions using phosphorus diffusion.
- Produce and characterize silicon dioxide layers for device insulation and gate oxides.
- Implement photolithography to pattern semiconductor structures.
- Deposit aluminum contacts using thermal evaporation.
- Electrically characterize fabricated devices using a probe station and source measurement equipment.
- Investigate CMOS fabrication as a stretch goal.

## Semiconductor Fabrication Process

The NMOS fabrication process being developed involves several major semiconductor manufacturing operations.

### 1. Silicon Wafer Preparation

The team begins with P-type silicon wafers that serve as the substrate for NMOS fabrication.

Wafer preparation includes cleaning and inspection to reduce contamination before high-temperature processing.

**Equipment and Processes:**
- Silicon wafers
- RCA cleaning
- Deionized water rinsing
- Solvent cleaning
- Wafer inspection



### 2. Thermal Oxidation

Silicon wafers are placed into a high-temperature tube furnace to grow a silicon dioxide (SiO2) layer.

This oxide serves several functions during fabrication, including protecting selected regions during processing and forming insulating layers within semiconductor devices.

The team has experimented with wet thermal oxidation and varying oxidation durations to develop appropriate oxide thicknesses.

One challenge has been achieving consistent oxide formation while preventing contamination and defects on the wafer surface.

**Equipment:**
- Carbolite Gero tube furnace
- Steam generation equipment
- Nitrogen gas system

### 3. Photolithography and Oxide Etching

Photolithography is used to create patterned regions on silicon wafers.

The process involves applying photoresist, exposing selected areas to ultraviolet light through a mask, and developing the patterned resist.

Wet chemical etching is subsequently used to remove exposed silicon dioxide where required.

This process allows the team to define specific regions for diffusion, gate formation, and electrical contacts.

**Equipment:**
- Spin coater
- Hot plate
- Ultraviolet light source
- Photolithography masks
- Developer and photoresist chemicals
- Wet etching equipment (HF-safe plastics) 
- Carbolite Gero tube furnace

### 4. Phosphorus Diffusion

Phosphorus diffusion is used to introduce N-type dopants into selected regions of P-type silicon wafers.

The objective is to create heavily doped N-type regions that can ultimately function as the source and drain of an NMOS transistor.

The process involves applying a phosphorus-based spin-on dopant and performing high-temperature diffusion.

The team has investigated several challenges associated with this process, including:

- Controlling dopant diffusion depth.
- Preventing excessive diffusion.
- Removing residual phosphorus-containing glass after diffusion.
- Verifying the electrical characteristics of doped silicon regions.

### 5. Aluminum Metallization

Aluminum is deposited onto fabricated semiconductor structures using a thermal evaporation system.

The purpose of this step is to create conductive contacts and, where appropriate, metal gate electrodes.

This process is important for electrically connecting fabricated devices to external testing equipment.

**Equipment:**

- Aluminum evaporation material

## Preliminary Device Fabrication and Testing

Before completing the full NMOS transistor fabrication process, the team has been developing and testing simpler semiconductor structures.

These devices allow us to evaluate individual fabrication steps before combining them into a functioning transistor.

### PN Junction Diodes

The team has fabricated experimental PN junction diodes using phosphorus diffusion.

Electrical testing has been performed to investigate their behavior and evaluate the effectiveness of the diffusion process.

Some devices have exhibited unexpected polarity and electrical characteristics, requiring further investigation into doping, contact formation, and measurement methods.

## Ellipsometer Development

In addition to semiconductor processing, I am leading a subteam focused on developing a low-cost ellipsometer for measuring silicon dioxide thickness.

Accurately measuring oxide thickness is essential for semiconductor fabrication, especially when developing thin gate oxides for MOSFET devices.

Commercial ellipsometers can be expensive, so our goal is to investigate an alternative measurement system using affordable optical components and custom-designed mechanical structures.

The project involves:

- Researching ellipsometry measurement principles.
- Investigating optical methods for determining thin-film thickness.
- Designing components suitable for 3D printing.
- Evaluating potential calibration and measurement methods.
- Developing a practical approach for characterizing oxidized silicon wafers.

This portion of the project is currently under development.


## Technical Challenges

Several significant challenges have been encountered during the development of the fabrication process.

### Wafer Contamination

Some thermal oxidation experiments have resulted in visible defects or contamination on the wafer surface.

Potential causes under investigation include wafer handling, cleaning effectiveness, compressed-air exposure, and contamination introduced during previous furnace operations.

### Dopant Diffusion Control

Achieving the appropriate diffusion depth and concentration is essential for producing functioning NMOS devices.

The team has investigated excessive diffusion and residual glass formation following high-temperature processing.

### Oxide Thickness and Quality

Developing a suitable gate oxide requires accurately controlling and measuring silicon dioxide thickness.

Oxide thickness also influences MOSFET electrical characteristics, making repeatable oxide growth particularly important.

### Electrical Characterization

Preliminary diode and MOS capacitor measurements have produced inconsistent results.

These experiments are being used to identify possible fabrication and testing issues before proceeding with complete NMOS devices.
